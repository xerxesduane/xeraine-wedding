Why Dubai, why now

The part of our story that starts the day after the wedding.
Our story

How we got here

It is a story about a prayer, a heartbreak, a hundred dollars, and one man in a crowd of five thousand. None of it went the way we planned.

One

A prayer nobody heard but God

In 2021 I finished seminary, was ordained, and went where the roads ran out. Island after island across the Philippines — discipleship, leadership training, teaching young believers how to carry a ministry online. Short trips to Thailand and Myanmar to teach whoever would gather.

It was full, and it was good. But there was one prayer I kept praying alone, on long boat rides and in borrowed rooms: Lord, give me someone to serve you with. Not someone to complete me — someone to be sent with.

Two

She bought the book for another man

Back in 2022 a woman bought a preaching book from me. Not for herself — for her boyfriend, a pastor, who would later betray her and break her heart in the worst way that kind of story ends.

I did not think of her again until I noticed something odd. Every time I posted from an outreach in the mountains, she was there. Liking. Watching. Following a work she had no reason to follow.

So I did the only thing I knew how to do: I presented the ministry to her and asked her to partner with it. She said yes — in prayer, and with her own money, out of a wound that was still healing. The first thing we ever built together was not a romance. It was a mission.

Then I found I wanted to know her. Not just her support — her heart, and what she thought God was doing in the world. So we prayed and we fasted, and we asked God plainly whether this was His idea. It was hard. There were walls on both sides, and good reasons for them. God took them down one by one, and then He said yes.

Three

The country I did not want

There was one problem. She had been working in the UAE for seven years. And I did not want to go.

I want to be honest about that, because people assume missionaries are always straining toward the next country. I was not. I had a community I loved, a ministry that was bearing fruit, and I was content — which is a harder thing to leave than unhappiness ever is.

But God kept stirring one thought I could not put down: go and be a friend to Muslims. Not a project. A friend. So I stopped arguing and started praying.

Four

A hundred dollars I did not have

Then came an email that made no sense. I had been nominated to attend the Fourth Lausanne Congress in South Korea — in person. Out of leaders across the entire world, a young minister who had barely started. I read it twice.

Then I read the fee. Two and a half thousand US dollars.

I did not have it. I had no path to it. What I had was a hundred-dollar deposit and a decision about what kind of man I wanted to be. I paid the hundred, and I prayed like it mattered.

Weeks later Lausanne wrote back. They were covering the whole cost. Every dirham of it. And the door I could not open from Manila — the one that makes leaving the Philippines for the Gulf so hard — opened from the other side.

Five

One man in a crowd of five thousand

Korea. More than five thousand delegates. And in all of that, I met Pastor Bhaveshkumar Nagda.

I did not know that he had been my fiancée's teacher in Tribes and Mission. I did not know he would introduce me to pastors serving in Dubai, or that one of those threads would lead to Pastor Bill Koogler at Fellowship Dubai. I flew to Korea for a conference. I came home with a country.

On 18 December 2024 I married Loraine. I moved to Dubai, found work, and served at Fellowship Dubai in whatever hours were left over — until August 2025, when it stopped being leftover hours and became the whole thing. In August 2026 we stood in Tagaytay and said it all again in front of the people we love. Some of them are reading this now.

Six

And this is the city we were sent to

Dubai runs on people who came from somewhere else. Nine out of ten of our neighbours were born in another country. They arrive for work, they send money home, and a great many of them are quietly lonely in a way a paycheque does not fix. We know. We were two of them.

It is the same conversation over and over — in break rooms, in car parks, on the metro at eleven at night. A man on a two-year contract who has not held his daughter since she was one. A nurse working doubles so her brother can finish school. Nobody to sit with. Nowhere to be known.

We started with a table and a rice cooker, and it turned out that was most of what was needed. Threshold is the name we gave it — the doorway somebody crosses when they stop being a stranger. Because far from home is exactly where people start asking the real questions, and somebody should be there when they do.

That is why the wedding matters to this. A home is a resource. A table with two people at it can seat ten. We did not marry each other to be comfortable.

“I paid the hundred dollars I had.
God paid the rest.”

Partnership

Four ways in, and none of them start with money

Choose the one that is honestly true for you right now. The last one is a real option, and plenty of people choose it.

Pray with us

You get our monthly letter, the real one, with the setbacks in it too. Most of what has worked here was prayed for by someone who never visited.

Give with us

Monthly or one time. It pays for the room, the food on the table, and the travel. You will always see where it went.

Come alongside

Design, accounting, a contact, one evening a month, a spare room for a visitor. The best help has often involved no money at all.

Just hear us out

Sit with us, ask the hard questions, and decide nothing that day. If the answer is no in the end, we would still rather have had the coffee.

Straight answers

The questions people actually ask

Is this a job, or volunteering?

Both, honestly. We work, and this is not a side hobby — it is where our evenings, our weekends and a serious share of our income go. Partnership is what lets us give it more hours rather than fewer.

Where does the money actually go?

The room, the food, transport and materials. We publish a simple breakdown in the monthly letter, and you can ask us anything about it directly.

Do I have to commit to anything?

No. There is no minimum and no fixed term, and there is no awkwardness if you stop. Seasons change, and we would rather keep the friendship than the pledge.

I am not religious. Is this for me?

You are welcome at the table, and welcome to hear about it. Plenty of people support the practical side of the work — the meals, the mentoring — without sharing our faith.

I do not live in the UAE.

Most of our partners do not. A video call works fine, and the letter travels well. If you are ever passing through Dubai, tell us — a layover counts as a visit.

Let's talk

Coffee, juice, or forty minutes on a call

No slides and no pressure. Pick a time that suits you, and if none of them do, just message us and we will work around it.
