The Long Version

How we got here

01

A city everyone passes through

Dubai runs on people who came from somewhere else. Nine out of ten of our neighbours were born in another country. They arrive for work, they send money home, and a great many of them are quietly lonely in a way that a paycheque doesn't fix. We know, because we were two of them.

It is one of the most spiritually open cities we have ever lived in — not because anyone is preaching on the corner, but because everybody here is far from home, and far from home is where people ask the real questions.

02

What we kept noticing

The same conversation, over and over, in break rooms and car parks and on the metro at 11pm. A man on a two-year contract who hasn't held his daughter since she was one. A nurse working doubles so her brother can finish school. Nobody to sit with. Nowhere to be known.

We started with a table and a rice cooker, and it turned out that was most of what was needed. Food, a chair, and somebody who remembers your name the second time.

03

Why the wedding matters to it

On 7 August we promised each other something in front of you. Part of that promise was that this marriage would not be built only for our own comfort. A home is a resource. A table with two people at it can seat ten.

Threshold is the name we gave it — the doorway people cross when they stop being a stranger. It's hospitality, mentoring, practical help, and the slow work of being consistently present in a city where almost nobody stays.

“We didn't want a wedding that was the biggest day of our lives.
We wanted one that was the smallest thing we'd build together.”

Partnership

Four ways in, and none of them start with money

Pick the one that's honestly true for you right now. The last one is a real option, and plenty of people choose it.

Pray with us

You get our monthly letter — the real one, wins and setbacks both. Most of what has worked here was prayed for by someone who never visited.

Give with us

Monthly or one-time. It goes to rent for the space, food for the table, and travel to the labour camps. You'll always see where it went.

Come alongside

Design, accounting, a network, an evening a month, a spare room for a visitor. Some of the best partnership has never involved money.

Just hear us out

Sit with us, ask the hard questions, decide nothing that day. If the answer ends up being no, we'd still rather have had the coffee.

Straight Answers

The questions people actually ask

Is this a job, or volunteering?

Both, honestly. We work, and the ministry is not a side hobby — it's where our evenings, weekends and a serious share of our income go. Partnership is what lets us give it more hours rather than fewer.

Where does the money actually go?

Space, food, transport, and materials. We publish a simple breakdown in the monthly letter — no fine print, and you can ask us anything about it directly.

Do I have to commit to anything?

No. There's no minimum, no term, and no awkwardness if you stop. People's seasons change and we'd rather keep the friendship than the pledge.

I'm not religious. Is this for me?

You're still welcome at the table, and you're welcome to hear about it. Plenty of people support the practical side of the work — the meals, the mentoring — without sharing our faith.

I don't live in the UAE.

Most of our partners don't. A video call works fine, and the monthly letter travels well. If you're ever transiting through Dubai, tell us — layovers count as visits.

Let's Talk

Coffee, juice, or forty minutes on a call

No slides, no pressure, no ask at the end unless you want one. Pick a time that suits you — and if none of them do, just message us and we'll work around it.
